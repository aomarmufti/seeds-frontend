# Seeds — Student Acquisition & Marketing Strategy

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Context:** pre-scale tutoring business, strong product/brand, weak funnel (see UX-REVIEW.md), founder's goal = sign up students now.

**Guiding principle:** at your stage, marketing = *trust transfer*. Parents don't buy tuition from websites; they buy it from people they trust. Every tactic below either borrows trust (communities, schools, reviews) or compounds it (referrals, proof).

---

## Tier 1 — Do this week (free, highest ROI)

1. **Google Business Profile.** This is #1. The landing claims "4.9★ · 120+ Google reviews" — if there isn't a real, review-rich profile at the other end, that's the first thing to build. Create/claim it, add photos, services, the booking link. Then start the **reviews flywheel**: after every successful month (or after lesson 5 and lesson 10), personally ask each current parent for a Google review — WhatsApp them the direct review link. 10 genuine reviews beats £1,000 of ads.
2. **Fix the funnel first (the website work from today).** Every marketing pound poured into a funnel with no signup, no pricing, and dead ends is wasted. Auth + pricing page + one clear CTA *before* any paid spend.
3. **Referral programme.** Your cheapest student is a current student's friend's sibling. Launch "Refer a family — you both get a free lesson". Announce it to every existing parent personally by WhatsApp, not just email. (Build: unique referral links in the portal — see Jira tickets.)
4. **Community presence (your unfair advantage).** The faith-integrated positioning gives you warm access to masjids, Islamic schools, madrassas, and Muslim parent WhatsApp/Facebook groups — channels MyTutor cannot enter credibly. Offer: a free "How to get your child from Grade 5 to Grade 8 in GCSE Maths" talk/workshop at 2–3 local masjids or community centres. Collect emails at the door. This is borrowed trust in its purest form.
5. **WhatsApp Business.** Put a WhatsApp click-to-chat on the site and use a Business account with a proper profile. UK parents overwhelmingly prefer it over email for this kind of purchase.

## Tier 2 — This month (low cost)

6. **Exam-season content SEO.** You have zero content surface; competitors rank because they publish. Highest-intent pages first, one per week, genuinely useful:
   - "GCSE Maths grade boundaries 2026 (Edexcel/AQA/OCR)"
   - "How to revise for GCSE Biology — a tutor's 8-week plan"
   - "A-Level Maths: the 10 topics that decide A vs A*"
   Each ends with the free-consultation CTA. These rank locally and convert warm.
7. **Local SEO pages.** "GCSE Maths tutor in [your city/area]" — even online-only tuition converts better with a local anchor; parents search "near me".
8. **School & teacher relationships.** Head of Year / careers leads at local schools: offer free revision workshops or a parents'-evening presence. Teachers can't recommend paid tutors officially, but they absolutely tell parents informally who's good.
9. **Social proof engine on Instagram/TikTok.** Short clips: a tutor solving one exam question beautifully in 60 seconds, "why π appears everywhere" wonder-hooks (your brand's natural content!). Post 3×/week. You're not chasing virality; you're building the profile a parent checks after hearing your name.
10. **Parent testimonials on video.** Two or three 30-second phone videos from current parents. Raw > polished. These go on the landing page and in ads later.

## Tier 3 — When the funnel converts (paid, carefully)

11. **Google Ads, high intent only.** Exact-match: "gcse maths tutor", "a level chemistry tutor", "+[city]" variants, "11+ tutor" if applicable. Send each to a matching landing page (not the homepage). Budget £10–15/day, kill anything without a booking in 30 clicks. A free-consultation offer converts cold traffic far better than "sign up".
12. **Meta ads to parents.** Lead with the video testimonials and the Grade 4→8 story. Target parents of 13–18-year-olds within your area. Use the lead-magnet (past-paper pack) as the offer, then nurture by email — cold parents rarely book a call from one ad.
13. **Tutor marketplaces as a funnel.** Profiles on Tutorful/MyTutor/Superprof where permitted — they rank on Google for the terms you want; some parents will find you there and you can build reputation that later feeds direct bookings (check each platform's terms about off-platform conversion).

## Always-on mechanics

- **Lead magnet + email nurture:** free "Past-Paper Pack by exam board" for an email address → automated 5-email sequence over 2 weeks (story → method → proof → objection handling → book a consultation). Captures the 95% who aren't ready to book today.
- **Follow-up SLA:** every wizard lead and consultation no-show gets a personal WhatsApp within 2 hours, 3 follow-ups over a week. Speed-to-lead is the single biggest conversion lever in services.
- **Seasonal campaigns:** September (new year, new targets), January (mock results panic — the biggest tutoring-buying moment of the year), March–May (exam countdown, "8-week grade rescue"), Ramadan (community goodwill + evening revision clubs), results day in August ("didn't go to plan? free consultation").
- **Track one number weekly:** consultations booked per week, and where each came from (ask "how did you hear about us?" in the booking form — add a field). Double down on whatever produces; stop what doesn't.

## Suggested 30-day sequence

| Week | Actions |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ship funnel fixes (auth/pricing/CTA) · Google Business Profile live · ask every current parent for a review |
| 2 | Referral programme announced personally · WhatsApp button on site · first masjid workshop booked |
| 3 | First 2 SEO articles live · 3 short-form videos posted · video testimonial collected |
| 4 | Google Ads pilot (£100 test) · lead magnet + email sequence live · review numbers, decide what scales |

**What NOT to do yet:** print/leaflets at scale, broad brand ads, a second subject expansion, a mobile app. Depth in one channel that works beats breadth everywhere.
